# Aquarium

When ForkMesh is not the active app, the whole desktop window turns into an
aquarium. Every agent is a fish with its name, status and changed files under
it, stopped and finished ones too. Its colour and the way it swims follow its
status (a stopped fish sleeps on the sand, a failed one wears a plaster, a
merged one has a purple rim), it grows with the files it changed, its fins with
the lines, and a merge conflict tangles it in red fishing line. The fish peek
at buttons, read headings letter by letter, surf progress bars, check list
rows, magnify text, carry small buttons about and do 30 such things with
what is on screen, and play 30 games with the Genie spider (tag, races, a
bubble crown, a dance party, a photo shoot and more). A fish whose agent is
waiting for you hovers near the top and asks for you. Come back to the window
and the water drains at once.

The **Aquarium** page in the rail turns it on and off, sets how long ForkMesh
must be in the background first (3 seconds by default), shows it on demand
with **Show it now**, shows every agent's fish, and keeps a log of the latest
tricks.

On the iPhone, turn it on with **Installed on this device** on the package's
Market page. The fish then swim over the app while it is open, one for each
running agent (three guests when none is running). They read headings, polish
buttons from beside them, measure and underline text, and play with the Genie
spider, but never sit on a button, a link, a switch or a logo, and every touch
goes straight through to the app.

Free, bundled with the desktop and the iPhone app, off by default. Developer notes:
