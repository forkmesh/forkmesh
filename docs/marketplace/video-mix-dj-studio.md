# Video Mix DJ Studio

`video-mix-dj-studio` is an account-synced Marketplace package with a responsive dashboard page at `/dashboard/video-mix-dj-studio`, a desktop rail link, and a native iOS route. The dashboard page's mobile-first deck is a fixed, non-scrolling surface: both turntables and their waveforms remain visible in portrait and landscape, while loading, search, mixer, sampler and set actions open compact overlays above it. Loaded video artwork becomes the spinning record label.

## iOS

The iOS route is a SwiftUI studio, not a web page (`native/ios/ForkMesh/Features/Packages/VideoMixDjStudio*.swift`, with its pure logic and tests in `ForkMeshCore/Sources/FMPackages/DjStudio.swift`). Opening the route presents it as a full-screen cover, so the shell's top bar, tab bar, prompt bar, spider and every other overlay are hidden along with the status bar and home indicator; leaving it (gear menu or the info sheet) pops the route again.

- **Landscape** follows the classic two-turntable layout: a top bar with each deck's elapsed time, title and channel, the menu, capture (record) and info buttons, and both waveforms; pitch strips on the outer edges (settings, BPM, SYNC, pitch fader, loop, − / + bend); the two platters with the pads button and AUTOMIX tab between them; and a bottom bar with the play buttons, SET / cue buttons and the crossfader.
- **Portrait** stacks Deck A above Deck B with the mixer (menu, capture, info, waveform zoom, pads, crossfader and AUTOMIX) between them.
- The iPhone app is otherwise portrait-only. `FmOrientationLock` (answered by `AppDelegate`'s `supportedInterfaceOrientationsFor`) allows landscape only while the studio is on screen and rotates back to portrait when it closes; iPad keeps every orientation.

Each deck plays through its own YouTube IFrame player in a WKWebView hosted on the server's origin (or `https://forkmesh.com` when signed out), so YouTube's embedding rules still apply. The player is shown inside the record, clipped to a circle and dimmed with the crossfader, and takes no touches: the native platter on top owns them. When the deck is paused or being scratched, the video's artwork is shown instead and turns with the platter. Position comes from a quarter-second player tick and is interpolated on device, so the platter, clock and waveform playhead move at display rate without extra JavaScript calls. A static view is redrawn only when its inputs change; the turntable, waveform and clock timelines pause while a deck is stopped.

- Dragging a record turns it like vinyl, 1.8 seconds of track per revolution, seeking at most every 80 ms and resuming playback on release if the deck was playing. The red light below the platter returns the deck to its cue and stops it.
- YouTube does not expose audio data, so the waveform strips are a stable picture generated from the video id: they show position and let you tap or drag to seek, and the waveform button zooms them to a 16-second window around the playhead.
- The pitch fader covers ±16 % (top is slower, double-tap resets), − / + bend the tempo by 4 % while held, and the BPM box is tap tempo: tap it on the beat, then SYNC matches this deck's BPM (or, without BPMs, its tempo) to the other deck.
- The crossfader is equal power; its arrows slam to one side and AUTOMIX starts the idle deck, fades to it over eight seconds and pauses the other. iOS ignores a web player's volume, so on iPhone a deck is muted rather than lowered once its side of the fader drops below 2 %.
- The record button captures eight seconds from the deck you can hear into one of four sections; the sampler sheet replays them and has the eight synthesized drum pads, rendered once into `AVAudioEngine` buffers and triggered on touch-down.
- Search uses the same public Invidious index as the web page, trying up to three healthy instances; a pasted YouTube link loads directly.
- Deck videos, titles, cues, tempo, BPM, loop, levels, sections, sampler level, crossfader and zoom are kept in `UserDefaults` (`fm.videoMixDjStudio.v1`) on the device only. While the studio is open the screen stays awake and the audio session mixes the decks with the sampler.

## Web and desktop

The studio accepts a YouTube URL or video id for each deck, detects the common watch, share, Shorts, live, embed, attribution and playlist forms, removes their extra parameters, embeds the canonical video through YouTube’s privacy-enhanced host, and uses the player message protocol for transport, position, tempo and volume. Its in-studio search gets results from a healthy public CORS-enabled Invidious index; selecting a result loads its canonical YouTube video directly into the chosen deck. Each overlay announces its current purpose and holds keyboard focus until it is closed, when focus returns to the control that opened it; selected search destinations and changing result states are also exposed to assistive technology. The equal-power crossfader sets each player’s effective volume and its matching opacity, so sound and picture make the same transition. Each deck’s touch platter pauses, seeks as it is rotated and resumes when released. Capture stores an eight-second source region as a local sampler section; its button seeks and plays the region back from its source deck. The local Web Audio sampler also has eight touch-safe synthesized percussion pads (mapped to `E`, `R`, `T`, `Y`, `U`, `I`, `O` and `P`), so it does not download, upload or retain audio recordings. Cues, captured regions, deck level, tempo, sampler level and the crossfader are retained only in `localStorage`; no package API or worker is present.

The page is intentionally controlled by the selected video’s availability policy. YouTube advertising, embedding rules and any playback restrictions remain under YouTube and the video owner’s control.
