# Video Mix DJ Studio

`video-mix-dj-studio` is an account-synced Marketplace package with a responsive dashboard page at `/dashboard/video-mix-dj-studio`, a desktop rail link, and a native iOS route. The dashboard page's mobile-first deck is a fixed, non-scrolling surface: both turntables and their waveforms remain visible in portrait and landscape, while loading, search, mixer, sampler and set actions open compact overlays above it. Loaded video artwork becomes the spinning record label.

## iOS

The iOS route is a SwiftUI studio, not a web page (`native/ios/ForkMesh/Features/Packages/VideoMixDjStudio*.swift`, with its pure logic and tests in `ForkMeshCore/Sources/FMPackages/DjStudio.swift`). Opening the route presents it as a full-screen cover, so the shell's top bar, tab bar, prompt bar, spider and every other overlay are hidden along with the home indicator. Since 2026-10-17 the iOS status bar (time, signal and battery) stays visible, so the battery level is in view during a set; iPhones hide it themselves in landscape. The back button in the top-left corner (also the info sheet) closes it in one tap and pops the route. There is no gear menu (removed 2026-10-16): every entry it had is one tap away elsewhere, loading on the library handles, the deck titles and the platter's load button, deck settings on each pitch strip, pads, Reset All and info in the bottom row, and Leave on the back button.

The studio model is app-wide (`DjStudioHub`), so leaving does not stop the music: playing decks keep playing and the shell's top bar shows a spinning record (`DjNowPlayingChip`, `VideoMixDjStudioNowPlaying.swift`) with the loudest playing deck's artwork and, in its centre, a small amplitude pulse (`DjMiniPulse`, 2026-10-17; it replaced a seven-bar waveform): a dot in the colour of the sound that grows and glows with the loudness at the playhead, read from the measured waveform (`DjStudio.amplitude`, interpolated between bars). Since 2026-10-16 the record has no Liquid Glass circle behind it and fills the bar's full 44-point height. Tapping it reopens the studio; long-press offers Stop the Music. Leaving with nothing playing hands the audio session back.

- **Landscape** follows the classic two-turntable layout: a top bar with the back button, each deck's elapsed time next to the song's full length (`01:23 / 04:56`, 2026-10-17; tapping the clock switches the large figure to the time left), title and channel, and both waveforms; pitch strips on the outer edges (settings, BPM, SYNC, pitch fader, − / + bend) with each deck's volume fader just inside them; the two platters with the **queue** between them; a bottom bar with each deck's START / PAUSE / STOP / RESTART and SET / cue / SAMPLE buttons around the crossfader; and, below it at the very bottom, the info, pads, playlists, AUTOMIX, reactions and Reset All buttons and a tiny diagnostics button (`DjUtilityRow`).
- **Portrait** stacks Deck A above Deck B with the queue strip (and, in a jam, the people row) and the crossfader between them and the same info / pads / playlists / AUTOMIX / reactions / Reset All row at the bottom; each deck has START / PAUSE / STOP / RESTART and SET / cue / SAMPLE down its left side and its volume fader between the platter and the pitch strip.
- **Deck volume** (`DjLevelFader`, added 2026-10-16): each deck has its own vertical fader with a speaker icon and the level in percent. Dragging it sets that deck's level (the same level as the deck settings sheet), double-tap returns it to 100 %, and it multiplies with the crossfader. Levels stay on each phone, also in a jam.
- Nothing sits in the middle except the queue (2026-10-16): the capture (red record) button, the waveform zoom button, the logo tab and the loop button under each pitch fader are gone. Looping stays in each deck's settings sheet.
- START plays a deck (or opens the loader when it is empty), PAUSE holds it where it is, STOP stops it and goes back to the beginning, and RESTART starts the song over from the beginning, so the transport is never a guess. PAUSE lights while a deck is paused mid-song and STOP while it is stopped at the start.
- SAMPLE is an arm button with three steps (`DjSampleStep`): the first tap starts sampling from the deck's position (REC, red), the second stops it and arms the sample (ARMED, blue), the third disarms it and plays the sample. The sample runs from the start tap to the stop tap (at least one second, at most 30; under a quarter second is discarded) and is also saved to the next of the four sampler pads. Playing it jumps the deck to the sample; a playing deck carries on from the sample's end, a stopped deck stops again when the sample ends.
- While the studio is on screen the app's interface sounds are silenced (`FmCuePlayer.quieten`, which also covers the Genie chimes) so only the music is heard; haptics remain.
- The iPhone app is otherwise portrait-only. `FmOrientationLock` (answered by `AppDelegate`'s `supportedInterfaceOrientationsFor`) allows landscape only while the studio is on screen and rotates back to portrait when it closes; iPad keeps every orientation.

Both decks play through YouTube IFrame players in one WKWebView (`DjPlayerHost`) hosted on the server's origin (or `https://forkmesh.com` when signed out), so YouTube's embedding rules still apply. The web view takes no touches and is parked behind the app's root view in the key window, so it keeps playing after the studio closes; the record shows the video's artwork, dimmed with the crossfader and turning with the platter.

YouTube's API only offers its own speed steps (0.25 apart) and iOS ignores a web player's volume, so a user script (`frameScript`, injected into the YouTube frames with `forMainFrameOnly: false`) works on the frame's `<video>` element directly, driven by `postMessage` from the host page:

- it sets `playbackRate` to the deck's tempo (with `preservesPitch` off, like vinyl) and puts it back whenever YouTube resets it, so the pitch fader, bend and SYNC change the music;
- once the crossfader or deck level drops below full, it routes the video through a Web Audio gain node, so the fader really fades. It only does this when the source is a `blob:` (MediaSource) stream, because a cross-origin stream would go silent; otherwise the old behaviour remains and the deck mutes below 2 %;
- while a record is being scratched it plays the track forward at the hand's speed and seeks back for backward strokes.

Position comes from a quarter-second player tick and is interpolated on device at the deck's real rate. The playhead only advances once YouTube reports the video playing (not when START is pressed), small drift is eased out and jumps over 0.3 s snap (`DjStudio.trackedPosition`), so the waveform playhead stays on the music. A static view is redrawn only when its inputs change; the turntable, waveform and clock timelines pause while a deck is stopped.

Since 2026-10-17 the record and the waveform are drawn from a display position (`DjDeck.shown`, `DjSpin` in `DjStudio.swift`) instead of the raw one, so START, PAUSE, STOP and seeks no longer jerk:

- like a turntable motor, the speed ramps toward the deck's rate (0.09 s time constant up, 0.18 s down) and the shown position eases toward the real one (0.2 s), so the quarter-second corrections from the player are never seen as steps;
- pressing START used to turn the record at a third of its speed until the music was really heard; since the record's angle was the eased song position, that pull held it to a nudge of about 14 degrees, so the record stood still until YouTube started (up to three seconds into an AUTOMIX). Since 2026-10-17 the record has its own motor (`DjSpin.turn` and `motor`, read by `DjDeck.turned`): it spins up to the deck's rate as soon as START is pressed and turns freely with it, while the waveform's playhead still waits for the audio. Only a held or scratched record follows the position; seeks, cues and song changes no longer jump the record, and a frame stalled under half a second (`DjSpin.hitch`) turns it on by the time missed;
- after PAUSE or STOP the record keeps animating for 1.5 s (`coasting`) while it spins down and settles on the position the music actually stopped at, instead of freezing and then jumping when the player's late report arrives;
- a jump of more than 0.6 s (a seek, cue, restart or new song) moves straight there, and while a record is held or scratched it follows the hand exactly. The clock still reads the real position.
- since 2026-10-17 (later the same day) a frame that arrives more than 0.1 s after the previous one (`DjSpin.gap`: the app was busy, or the record was just set moving) puts the record and the waveform straight onto the position extrapolated from the player's last report, instead of standing still for that frame and then easing after the music for a fifth of a second; and the record and waveform timelines no longer set a 1/60 s minimum interval, which on a 60 Hz screen let timing jitter skip every so often a frame (so the spin stuttered at 30 fps for a moment). They now draw on every display frame while the deck moves (the record no longer uses a timeline at all; see Responsiveness);
- START sends the play command to the player before the deck's bookkeeping (play count, history, jam share, save), so nothing local stands between the tap and the audio.

- Touching a record only slows it, like a fingertip on vinyl: the deck's tempo drops 8 % while the finger rests there and for at least 0.35 s after a quick tap (`touchBegan` / `touchEnded`, `DjStudio.touchBrake`). A scratch starts only once the finger has moved 14 points (or 5 % of the platter, whichever is larger; `DjStudio.isScratch`), so a tap never jumps the track.
- Dragging a record scratches the song itself like vinyl, 1.8 seconds of track per revolution. Since 2026-10-17 the audio decoded for the waveform (22.05 kHz mono) is also kept on the deck as 16-bit samples (`DjScratchAudio`, songs up to 15 minutes, about 2.6 MB a minute; read again when a song whose waveform is cached is loaded). While the record is held the web voice is paused and one `DjScratchVoice` per deck on the sampler's `AVAudioEngine` plays those samples from the hand's position: a critically damped follower (`DjScratchHead`, 22 Hz) moves its read head after the finger, so forward strokes play the music forward and backward strokes play it in reverse, at the hand's speed and pitch, scaled by the crossfader and the deck's level. Holding the record still is silent, and releasing seeks the web voice to where the hand left the record and resumes it if the deck was playing. The muted video is moved to the hand's position four times a second. Before this the web voice could only play forward (seeking back on backward strokes), and what was heard was band-passed noise.
- Until a deck's audio is read (or when the stream cannot be read: WebM, HLS, Low Data Mode, songs over 15 minutes) the older scratch remains: the hand's speed (`DjStudio.scratchSpeed`) drives the web voice forward and a synthesized voice (band-passed noise whose pitch follows the speed, `DjScratchNoise`) plays, louder on backward strokes. The sampler level now sets only the drum pads' volume, not the scratch's. The red light below the platter returns the deck to its cue and stops it.
- The waveforms always scroll with the music: each strip is a 16-second window centred on the playhead, and dragging it scrubs the deck. They are drawn only from the real audio. When the frame script creates a deck's voice it reports the stream URL (`source` message, plain streams only, never HLS); `DjWaveSource` downloads that file once (not on Low Data Mode, at most 200 MB) in 2 MB `Range` requests, four at a time (`DjWaveRanges`, 2026-10-17: YouTube can throttle a single request for a whole stream to about playback speed, so the real waveform could arrive minutes late), decodes its audio track with `AVAssetReaderAudioMixOutput` at 22.05 kHz mono and `DjWaveAccumulator` turns it into 20 bars a second (at least 1200, at most 12,000; `DjWaveAccumulator.bars`), fine enough for the record's pulse to follow the beat: height is the loudness (RMS, normalised to the loudest bar) and colour is the balance of bass (below 250 Hz, red), mids (green) and treble (above 2.5 kHz, blue). The bars are cached per video in `Caches/dj-waveforms`; the downloaded file is a `dj-wave-<uuid>` copy in the temporary directory, deleted once decoded (its single-file removes are on the destructive-actions contract test's allow-list). While it is being read the strip says "Reading the audio…"; when the stream cannot be read (WebM, MSE or HLS) it shows only the centre line and playhead. The pattern that used to be generated from the video id was removed on 2026-10-17, because it looked like a waveform but had nothing to do with the music.
- The pitch fader covers ±16 % (pushing it up speeds the deck up, double-tap resets), − / + bend the tempo by 4 % while held, and the BPM box is tap tempo: tap it on the beat, then SYNC matches this deck's BPM (or, without BPMs, its tempo) to the other deck. Without a tapped BPM the box shows the tempo change as a percentage.
- The crossfader is equal power and fades through the Web Audio gain described above; its arrows slam to one side.
- **AUTOMIX** is an on / off mode (2026-10-16; it used to be a one-shot fade). While it is on, a 0.5 s loop asks `DjAutomix.step` (`ForkMeshCore/Sources/FMPackages/DjParty.swift`) what to do: with nothing playing it starts a cued deck (and swings the crossfader to it) or loads the next queued video onto the deck the crossfader favours; while a deck plays it preloads the next queued video onto the idle deck, so it starts at once; and when the playing song has eight seconds left (at its real tempo, plus one second for the other deck to start) it starts the other deck, crossfades to it over the time left (2 to 8 s) and pauses the old one. Since 2026-10-17 the crossfade waits until the incoming deck is really heard (it is advancing, at most `DjAutomix.audible`, 3 s) and then fades over the outgoing song's time left (2 to 8 s), and the mix begins with ten seconds left instead of nine (`DjAutomix.lead` is 2 s), so a slow YouTube start no longer fades the crowd into silence while the old song runs out. Loading from the queue takes the video off it, so the queue plays one song after another. A deck counts as cued when it is loaded, stopped and more than ten seconds from its end, so a song that just finished is never replayed. PAUSE or STOP that leaves nothing playing (or a jam partner pausing everything) turns AUTOMIX off, as do Stop the Music and Reset All. Holding AUTOMIX offers **Mix to the Other Deck Now**, the old eight-second fade. To keep the mix smooth (2026-10-17): the crossfader moves on every display frame (every 16 ms instead of 30 ms), the incoming song's waveform and scratch audio are not downloaded until the fade ends, so that download does not compete with the stream that is just starting, and a deck's audio fader no longer gives up on its Web Audio gain when the frame's audio context is still waking: only one wake-up is counted at a time, where every message during a fade used to count one, so a fade could fall back after a few hundredths of a second to muting below 2 % and cut the song in and out at full volume.
- **Shake to mix** (2026-10-17): shaking the phone while the studio is open (and no sheet is up) mixes into the next song, the same crossfade AUTOMIX makes: into the other deck if it is playing or cued, otherwise after loading the next queued video onto it (or, with the queue empty, a song from the played-songs library). It ticks and names the song it mixes into; with nothing playing it says to start a deck first, and it does nothing while a fade runs or a record is being scratched. The shake is UIKit's own gesture: a `UIWindow` extension (`VideoMixDjStudioScreen.swift`) posts `fmDeviceShaken` and passes the motion on, so shake to undo still works, and the studio listens for it only while it is on screen. In a jam AUTOMIX runs on the phone that turned it on; its loads, starts and pauses are shared like any other deck change.
- **The AUTOMIX button** (2026-10-17, `DjAutomixButton`) is a capsule the height of its round neighbours in the bottom row (28 points at the base scale, in landscape and portrait alike), with a shuffle icon before the label (two arrows while a crossfade runs). Off, it is the dark control face with a thin blue outline; on, it fills with the accent blue, the label turns dark and it glows. Its tap area is at least 44 by 44 points. It replaced a slanted tab (`DjTabShape`) with a small status dot, which read as a tab rather than a switch.
- **AUTOMIX never runs dry** (2026-10-17): when the queue is empty it loads a song from everything played before (the played-songs library below), so it keeps mixing after both decks' songs are over, for as long as it stays on. The pick (`DjAutomix.next`) leaves out the songs on either deck, shuffles among the older half of the rest by when each was last played, so the songs just heard are not repeated soon, and falls back to anything not playing when only those are left (one song played so far replays). It is preloaded onto the idle deck while the other plays, exactly like a queued video, so the change is as seamless as the queue's. A song that failed to play (removed, private, not embeddable) is skipped for the rest of the session, and a deck whose song failed counts as empty, so AUTOMIX replaces it instead of retrying it every half second. Since 2026-10-17 the pool also holds the songs saved in Playlists, and AUTOMIX can be kept to one playlist or one tag (see Playlists and tags below).
- The sampler sheet replays the four samples and has the eight synthesized drum pads, rendered once into `AVAudioEngine` buffers and triggered on touch-down.
- Search uses the same public Invidious index as the web page, trying up to three healthy instances. Loading is one step: type and press return, and the top result loads straight onto the deck and the sheet closes; a pasted YouTube link loads directly. Reopening the loader shows the other results (the loaded one ticked) to swap in a different video.
- Every song a deck starts playing goes to the top of **Recently Played** in the loader (the latest 40, no duplicates, titles filled in once YouTube reports them; `DjStudio.remember`). With the search box empty it is the first list, so a past song reloads in one tap; swipe a row to remove it or use Clear. It is kept with the rest of the studio state on the device.
- **Play counts** (2026-10-17): every song played is also kept in a played-songs library (`DjPlayedSong`: id, title, author, plays, last played; the latest 500, `DjStudio.played`). A play is counted when a deck starts a song it has not counted since it was loaded, stopped back to the start, restarted or played to the end, so PAUSE and START, a resume after the app comes back, a relaunch that carries on mid-song, a web-view reload and a jam correction never count twice. Recently Played rows, search results and the deck sheets show the count ("12 plays"), and a **Most Played** section (songs played more than once, most first, 25 at most) follows Recently Played. Swiping a song away in either list, or Clear, also removes it from the library, so AUTOMIX stops replaying it. State saved before the library existed starts it from Recently Played, one play each.
- Deck videos, titles, cues, tempo, BPM, levels, samples, sampler level, crossfader, the queue, the Recently Played history and the played-songs library are kept in `UserDefaults` (`fm.videoMixDjStudio.v1`) on the device only. Since 2026-10-17 the state is encoded and written on a serial background queue (`DjStore`), so the save every five seconds while a deck plays never holds up a frame on the main thread; going to the background or closing waits for the queue to finish. While the studio is open the screen stays awake and the audio session mixes the decks with the sampler.
- **Resuming the music** (2026-10-17): the saved state also holds each deck's position, whether it was playing and whether AUTOMIX was on. Each deck has a playing intent (`keepsPlaying`), set by START (and RESTART, AUTOMIX, a jam start) and cleared by PAUSE, STOP, the red cue light, Stop the Music, Reset All, a new load, a playback error or the song ending, so a pause YouTube or iOS makes on its own does not count as stopping. The state is written on every start and pause, every five seconds while a deck plays, and when the app goes to the background or is closed.
  - When the app is launched again, the shell (`HomeShell`, `DjStudioHub.relaunch`) checks the saved state once; if a deck was playing and the package is still installed it creates the studio in the background, loads each deck at its saved position and starts the decks that were playing (with AUTOMIX and the remembered jam, if any). The top-bar record appears as usual, and long-press still offers Stop the Music. A deck that was loaded but not playing comes back paused at its position instead of its cue.
  - When the app comes back to the foreground, or an audio interruption such as a phone call ends with iOS's resume hint, every deck that should be playing but is not is started again from where it is.
  - If the web view's content process is killed (iOS does this to background web views under memory pressure), `DjPlayerHost.onReload` has the model reload each deck at its current position and play the ones that should be playing, instead of starting them from where they were first loaded.

### Playlists and tags

Added 2026-10-17 (`ForkMeshCore/Sources/FMPackages/DjCrate.swift`, tests in `DjCrateTests.swift`; views in `VideoMixDjStudioCrate.swift`, model glue in `VideoMixDjStudioPlaylists.swift`). The list button in the bottom row, between pads and AUTOMIX, opens **Playlists**:

- **AUTOMIX** at the top chooses what AUTOMIX plays once the queue is empty (`DjCrate.focus`): **Everything** (as before: every played song plus every saved one, shuffled toward the songs heard least recently), one **playlist** or one **tag**. With a playlist or tag, **Shuffle** picks among its songs the same way, and with Shuffle off it plays them in order from the song after the last one it picked (`cursor`) and starts over at the end. The queue always plays first. Songs that failed to play are skipped. Choosing a focus while AUTOMIX is on reloads an idle deck whose song is outside it, so the next mix is already from the list. Holding AUTOMIX also offers the choice (**Plays …**) and a link to Playlists, and its shuffle icon becomes a target while a focus is set.
- **Playlists**: create, rename and delete; each page lists its songs with Edit to reorder or remove them, **Add Songs** (tick songs from everything played or saved, or paste a YouTube link into the filter), **Play with AUTOMIX** (sets the focus and turns AUTOMIX on), **Add All to the Queue** and **Shuffle into the Queue** (in a jam, into the jam's queue).
- **Tags**: free words such as `chill` or `warm up`, lowercased, without `#`, up to 20 per song and 32 characters each. A tag page lists its songs and has the same play and queue actions; a tag can be renamed (renaming onto an existing tag merges them) or deleted, and a song swiped off. **All Songs** lists every played or saved song with a filter (title, channel or `#tag`); removing a song there takes it out of every playlist, drops its tags and its play count.
- Every song row is a menu: Load on Deck A / B, Add to the Queue, Add to Playlist and **Tags and Playlists…**, a page that toggles the song's tags (or adds new ones, separated by commas) and its playlists. The same page opens from holding a song in the loader (Recently Played, Most Played or a search result, which also offers Add to Playlist), from a queue thumbnail and from the deck settings sheet.
- **Filing from the turntable** (2026-10-17, `DjFileButton`): a loaded deck has a list button in the platter's bottom-right corner (opposite the red cue light, clear of the tonearm once a song is loaded). It lights blue when the song is already in a playlist or has a tag. Its menu lists every playlist and tag (ticked where the song already is), New Playlist… and New Tag…, Tags and Playlists…, and, when the song belongs to the list AUTOMIX is focused on, Take Out of that list. Choosing a list while AUTOMIX is focused on a playlist or tag that holds the song *moves* it (`DjStudioModel.move`): it is added to the chosen list and taken out of the focused one (removed from that playlist, or that tag taken off); otherwise the song is only added and keeps playing (a song that was never in the focused list is not skipped for being filed). If AUTOMIX is on and the song has left the focused list, it is skipped (`skipOut`): a playing deck crossfades over two seconds into the other deck, which is first loaded with the next song from the queue or the list if it does not already hold one, and once the fade ends the old deck is loaded with the following song (or emptied when there is none), so AUTOMIX never comes back to the moved song; with both decks playing the deck simply pauses and the crossfader swings to the other; an idle deck holding the song is reloaded. With nothing else to play the deck pauses. During a running crossfade or a scratch the song is filed but not skipped.
- **Next on the turntable** (2026-10-18, `DjNextButton`, `DjStudioModel.quickNext`): every platter has a skip-forward button on its bottom edge, beside the red cue light. It loads the next song onto that deck, exactly the song AUTOMIX would pick: the first queued video (taken off the queue), otherwise the next song of the AUTOMIX focus (Everything, a playlist, a tag or a smart playlist, shuffled or in order), never a song already on a deck or one that failed to play. A playing deck starts the new song at once; a paused or empty deck is cued. Pressed during an AUTOMIX crossfade it stops the fade, swings the crossfader back to the other deck if that one is playing and cues the new song instead. With nothing to load it says so. The deck settings sheet also has Load the next song.
- **Find similar** (2026-10-18, `DjSimilarButton`, `DjSimilarSheet` in `VideoMixDjStudioSimilar.swift`): a loaded deck has a sparkle magnifier on the platter's bottom edge, opposite Next (also in the file menu and the deck settings sheet). It opens suggestions to load on the next deck: the picker starts on the other deck (or Queue when the other deck is playing, so the music is not cut) and a tap loads the song there and closes the sheet; holding a row offers either deck, the queue or a playlist. **From Your Library** ranks played and saved songs by what they share with the deck's song (`DjSmart.similar`): the same artist (5), each shared tag (3), each playlist holding both (2), the same genre (2), style or decade (1), with the reasons shown on the row. **Suggested on YouTube** is the video's own recommendations from the same Invidious instances the search uses (`/api/v1/videos/<id>?fields=recommendedVideos`, `DjStudio.similarResults`); when that fails or is empty it searches for the artist and genre, or the title without its brackets (`DjSmart.searchQuery`). Songs on either deck are left out, and the suggestions are fetched once per song.
- **Don't Shuffle** (2026-10-18): every song menu (the turntable's file button, song rows, holding a song in the loader) has a Don't Shuffle toggle, and Tags and Playlists and the deck settings sheet have Leave Out of Shuffle. Such a song (`DjTrack.noShuffle`, saved in the collection) is never picked at random: AUTOMIX on Everything, a shuffled playlist, tag or smart playlist, Next and shake to mix leave it out (`DjCrate.pool`), while a playlist played in order, the queue and loading it by hand still play it. Rows show "Not shuffled". The flag is exported and imported with the song (`noShuffle: true`, written only when set).
- **Deleting songs** (2026-10-18): Delete from Library is in every song menu and the loader's hold menu (All Songs, Recently Played and Most Played also keep their swipe), and the turntable's file menu and deck settings sheet delete the loaded song. Deleting takes the song out of the library, every playlist, its tags, its play count and Recently Played, so neither AUTOMIX nor Next plays it again. On a deck it also takes the song off the local queue and replaces it at once: with AUTOMIX on a playing deck mixes into the other deck over two seconds (`skipOut`), otherwise the deck loads the next song (and keeps playing if it was) or is emptied when there is none.
- **Smart playlists** (2026-10-18, `ForkMeshCore/Sources/FMPackages/DjSmart.swift`, tests in `DjSmartTests.swift`; views `DjSmartKindView` and `DjSmartView`): Playlists has a Smart Playlists section that sorts every played and saved song automatically, recomputed whenever the library or the collection changes (`DjStudioModel.refreshSmart`, kept in `DjCrate.smart`, never saved). **Listening**: Most Played (more than one play, 50 at most), Recently Played (50), Never Played (saved, no plays), Played Once and Rediscover (last played over 30 days ago). **Genres** (house, techno, trance, drum & bass, dubstep, EDM, UK garage, hip-hop, R&B, pop, K-pop, rock, chill / lo-fi, Latin, Afrobeats, reggae, disco / funk, jazz, classical, country) and **Styles** (remixes and edits, DJ sets, live, acoustic and covers, sped up / slowed, official videos, lyric videos) match whole words of the title and tags, so a tag files a song into a genre its title does not name. **Decades** come from a year (1950 to 2029) or `80s`-style words, and **Artists** group songs by channel (without ` - Topic`, `VEVO`, ` Official` or ` Music`), or the part of the title before ` - ` when there is no channel, for every artist with two songs or more (30 at most). A list needs two songs. A smart playlist page has the same Play with AUTOMIX and queue actions as a playlist, plus Save as Playlist (an editable copy) and Export; AUTOMIX can be focused on one from Playlists or by holding AUTOMIX (`DjFocus.smart`, saved as `smart:<id>`; it falls back to Everything when the list empties). Filing a song while AUTOMIX plays a smart playlist only adds it, since nothing can be taken out of a smart list.
- **Export**: **Export Everything** (and Export in a playlist's or tag's menu) shares a JSON file (`format: forkmesh-dj-playlists`, `version: 1`, the songs with title, channel and tags, and the playlists by name with their video ids). **Export as M3U** shares the same songs as an M3U list of YouTube links for other apps.
- **Import**: **Import from Files** reads that JSON, an M3U playlist, any text with YouTube links (one per line) or a JSON list of links or objects with `videoId` / `url` and `title`; **Paste** reads the clipboard the same way. Imports merge: a song already saved keeps its tags and gains the imported ones, a playlist with the same name (ignoring case) gets the new songs appended, and links without tags or playlists become a playlist named after the file (or Pasted Songs).

Limits: 2,000 saved songs, 100 playlists, 1,000 songs a playlist, files up to 4 MB. Tagging or adding a song to a playlist saves it (id, title, channel); titles are filled in when YouTube reports them. The collection is kept on the device in its own `UserDefaults` key (`fm.videoMixDjStudio.crate.v1`), written on the save queue only when it changes, so the five-second deck save does not re-encode it. Reset All and Clear in Recently Played leave it alone; playlists are not shared in a jam, only the songs queued from them.

### Queue and jam sessions

The middle of the studio is a queue of small video thumbnails (`DjQueueView`, `VideoMixDjStudioQueue.swift`). + opens the loader with **Queue** chosen (the loader's picker is Deck A / Deck B / Queue), and the top search result or a tapped result is added instead of loaded. Tapping a thumbnail offers Load on Deck A, Load on Deck B or Remove; loading takes it off the queue. On its own the queue is kept on the device.

Sharing a YouTube link to ForkMesh from the iOS share sheet (2026-10-17) adds the video to the queue: when everything shared is YouTube video links (or the shared text is one such link) and there are no images, the share extension saves it and opens the studio without showing its sheet, and `SurfacesCoordinator.pickUpShare` queues each video (`DjStudio.sharedVideoIds`), into the jam's queue when one is joined. With the package removed the link goes to the agent composer as before.

Thumbnails can also be dragged onto a deck (2026-10-16). Holding one for 0.2 s lifts it (a haptic tick), a copy follows the finger with "Drop on a deck" or "Load on Deck A/B" under it, both platters show a dashed ring and the one under the finger (within 28 points of it) lights up; letting go there loads the video on that deck and takes it off the queue, anywhere else puts it back. A quick swipe still scrolls the queue, because the long press fails as soon as the finger moves. The drag is a SwiftUI gesture in the studio's coordinate space (`DjStudioView.space`), not a system drag session, so it starts quickly and works inside the full-screen cover; each platter reports its frame with `onGeometryChange` (`deckFrames`).

The people button at the top of the queue opens the jam sheet. **Start a Jam Session** creates a session on the server with the decks as they are; the host invites ForkMesh users by name (suggestions come from the chat user search, `/api/chat/direct-messages/users`), and they get a ping and see the invitation, marked by a red dot on the people button, the next time they open the studio. Once someone joins:

- the queue shown is the jam's, and anyone in the jam can add or remove videos, so the thumbnails show who added each one;
- both decks are shared. Every load, START, PAUSE, STOP, RESTART, seek, cue jump, tempo change, SYNC and scratch release is sent as that deck's state (video, playing, position, tempo), debounced by 250 ms (`share`). Each phone polls the jam every 2.5 s while the studio is open or a deck is playing, and follows a deck changed by someone else: it loads the same video, matches the tempo, seeks when it is more than 1.5 s off (0.5 s when paused) using the server's clock, and starts or pauses. A deck someone else is changing is not echoed back, and a local change still being sent wins over an older remote one;
- volume, deck levels, the crossfader, bend, the touch brake and samples stay on each phone, so everyone mixes what they hear;
- every phone plays its own copy of the songs: nothing is streamed from one phone to another. Since 2026-10-16 the poll is a `POST /jams/<id>/presence` (every 1.5 s while the studio is open, 2.5 s while it plays in the background), and half the request's round trip is added to the server clock before a deck's expected position is worked out, so a follower lands closer to the host;
- **people icons** (`DjPeopleStrip`, `VideoMixDjStudioParty.swift`): everyone in the jam has a round icon with their initial in a colour from their name, in the queue column (landscape) or a row under the queue (portrait), and in the jam sheet. The ring is the live status (`DjPersonStatus`): green in the studio, blue with headphones listening in the background, grey not listening, dimmed with a moon when nothing has been heard from them for 12 s, dashed while invited; the host has a crown. Your own icon uses your own state straight away;
- **motion**: while you are in a jam with the studio open or music playing, `DjMotionFeed` reads the accelerometer's user acceleration (gravity removed) 30 times a second, turned to the screen's orientation. Each presence post carries the strongest of every few samples (12 pairs, ±2 g) and an energy figure (RMS); other phones replay the trace on that person's icon until the next post, so it shakes, tilts and swells when they dance (energy 0.3 or more adds a dancing badge and "dancing" to its spoken label). Your own icon follows your phone live. **Share My Phone's Motion** in the jam sheet turns it off (`fm.videoMixDjStudio.shareMotion`, on by default); no location is ever sent. Reduce Motion keeps the icons still;
- **reactions**: the smiley button in the bottom row opens a bar of 30 reactions (`DjReaction.all`: hearts, fire, applause, confetti, fireworks, 100, LOL, star-struck, mind blown, lift off, sparkles, music, headphones, dance, groove, hands up, thumbs up, cool, lightning, disco, rainbow, balloons, snow, make it rain, feels, eyes, dead, drop, rock on and love it). Each has one of ten animation styles (rise, rain, burst, zoom, spin, orbit, wave, confetti, fireworks, flash) and plays for 1.6 to 3 seconds over the whole studio, with the sender's name at the top and their emoji above their icon. They are silent (no sound is played, so they never cover the music) and the flash styles pulse gently, under three times a second; with Reduce Motion every reaction is a single fade. Your reaction shows at once and is sent with `POST /jams/<id>/reactions`; the others see it on their next poll (reactions younger than 8 s are shown, each once). Outside a jam reactions still play on your own screen. They are drawn by one `Canvas` with the emojis as resolved symbols inside a `TimelineView` that only runs while a reaction is on screen, so they cost nothing the rest of the time;
- the host can remove people or end the jam for everyone; anyone else can leave. The current jam is remembered on the device (`fm.videoMixDjStudio.jam`) and rejoined when the studio opens.

The server side is the package's worker (`content/worker/video_mix_dj_studio.py`, `/api/video-mix-dj-studio`, `dataLifecycle.scope: account`). Its tables are `vmdj_jams`, `vmdj_jam_members` (owner / joined / invited), `vmdj_jam_queue`, `vmdj_jam_decks` (one row per side, so two people changing different decks never overwrite each other), `vmdj_jam_presence` (one row per person: last seen, state `studio` / `listening` / `idle`, playing, energy and motion; rows older than 30 s are not returned) and `vmdj_jam_reactions` (kind and sender, kept for 12 s, at most eight per person in that time, otherwise `too_many_reactions`, 429). Titles, queue items, deck states and presence are sealed with `runtime.seal`; a reaction's kind is a short id (`[a-z0-9-]{1,24}`) the app maps to its catalogue. A jam's reply carries `presence` and `reactions` (with `ago`, milliseconds since, so phones need no clock agreement) only for members who have joined. Routes:

| Route | Who |
| --- | --- |
| `GET /jams`, `POST /jams` | list my jams and invitations; create (up to 20 owned) |
| `GET /jams/<id>`, `DELETE /jams/<id>` | read; the host ends it, anyone else leaves |
| `POST /jams/<id>/join`, `POST /jams/<id>/leave` | an invitee joins; a member leaves |
| `POST /jams/<id>/members`, `DELETE /jams/<id>/members/<name>` | members invite (up to 32 people); the host removes |
| `POST /jams/<id>/queue`, `DELETE /jams/<id>/queue/<item>` | members add (up to 100) and remove |
| `PUT /jams/<id>/decks/<a or b>` | members set a deck's state |
| `POST /jams/<id>/presence` | members report their state and motion; returns the jam (the poll) |
| `POST /jams/<id>/reactions` | members send a reaction |

Invitees see the jam's people but not its queue or decks until they join. Leaving or being removed deletes your presence row. Deleting the account (or removing the package) ends the jams it hosts and drops its memberships, presence and reactions (`purge_account_data`).

### Responsiveness

Changes on 2026-10-16 so touching the decks never stutters:

- the platter's gesture bookkeeping (the last angle and whether a finger is down) lives in a plain object (`DjTouchTrack`) instead of `@State`, so a drag no longer re-evaluates the whole platter on every touch sample;
- the platter's spoken value is Playing / Paused / Empty instead of the clock, and the transport only reads the position while a deck is stopped, so the quarter-second player ticks no longer redraw the platter and transport of a playing deck (the clock, waveform, record and tonearm still follow the music);
- artwork is cut to a circle once when it loads (`DjArtwork`), so the spinning record and the top-bar record no longer clip a 640-pixel image every frame;
- button haptics use one prepared `UIImpactFeedbackGenerator` (`DjTheme.tick`), not a new generator per tap, so a press is felt the moment it lands;
- a jam poll only replaces the shared jam (people, queue, decks) when it changed; presence goes to its own `crowd` list, so the queue thumbnails are not rebuilt every 1.5 s, and a drag only redraws the queue when it starts and ends (`draggedId`);
- the notice bubble animates inside `DjNotice` instead of the studio's root view.

Since 2026-10-17 the spinning records are Core Animation layers instead of a SwiftUI timeline (`DjSpinner` and `DjSpinView`, `VideoMixDjStudioSpin.swift`), because re-evaluating the record's views on every frame on the main thread let any busy moment (a waveform redraw, a player tick, a jam poll) skip frames of the spin:

- the artwork, the rim dots and the label are one layer tree that is rotated as a whole. The dots and the label are drawn into a single bitmap once per platter size, so each frame changes only one transform;
- while a deck plays at a steady speed (the record's motor within 0.0005 of the deck's rate, `DjSpinView.settled`), the spin is a repeating `CABasicAnimation` that the render server runs by itself, so a busy main thread cannot stall it. A `CADisplayLink` checks at 15 Hz that the animation still matches `DjDeck.turned`, and restarts it from the model's angle if they drift apart by more than 0.01 rad (`DjSpinView.slip`) or if iOS removed it (for example in the background);
- spinning up or down, touch braking, bend, pitch changes and scratches are driven frame by frame from `DjDeck.turned` at up to 120 Hz. A change of rate, touch or scratch state wakes the view at once (`updateUIView`), so the switch away from the free-running animation never waits for the slow tick;
- the app now sets `CADisableMinimumFrameDurationOnPhone`, so ProMotion iPhones can run at 120 Hz (iPads already could). The waveform and reaction timelines are held to 60 Hz on those screens (`DjTheme.frame`, a 1/80 s minimum interval, which still draws every frame of a 60 Hz screen), so the main-thread work of the studio does not double.

Since 2026-10-18 the studio measures itself while it is on screen (`DjVitals`, `VideoMixDjStudioDiagnostics.swift`): frame times from a 60 Hz probe, the main-thread time of each piece of studio work, player tick gaps and drift, web view script round trips and what the user did around each long frame. The tiny stethoscope at the end of the bottom row (red with a warning triangle when the last 30 s had a freeze, steady stutter or a silent player) opens Studio diagnostics: a live read-out and Send to an agent, which starts a coding agent with that bundle and the app's performance report. The budgets, how to read the bundle and every check so far are in [video-mix-dj-studio-performance.md](video-mix-dj-studio-performance.md); each agent adds its check there and makes the studio collect what it was missing. The same change stopped a crossfade from scheduling a save every frame (it saves once at the end), sends both decks' volumes in one web view script, and gives each person in a jam's people row its own timeline instead of rebuilding the whole row 24 times a second.

### Why both decks can play at once

On iOS, WebKit lets only one `<video>` with sound play at a time across the whole app: starting one pauses every other one (`MediaSessionManageriOS::resetRestrictions` gives the `VideoAudio` media type `ConcurrentPlaybackNotPermitted`). The same type is also paused when the app goes to the background. A muted `<video>` is the `Video` type and an `<audio>` element is always the `Audio` type (`HTMLMediaElement::hasVideo` is only true for `<video>`), and neither has that rule.

Build 260 still stopped one deck when the other started. Its fix (2026-10-16) moved each deck's video track onto a hidden decoy `MediaSource`, which only works when YouTube streams through Media Source Extensions; when YouTube used a plain or HLS stream the deck's element still had video and sound, so the rule applied. The decoy also never played, so its buffer was never released, which made YouTube rebuffer and jump back (the "looping"). Since 2026-10-16 (second fix) the frame script makes the restriction impossible to hit:

- every YouTube `<video>` is kept muted for real. YouTube still sees its own mute setting, because `muted` is wrapped to report what YouTube last set. The mute is applied before any `play()` and again on `loadstart`, so no YouTube element can ever be `VideoAudio`;
- `MediaSource` and `ManagedMediaSource` are hidden inside the YouTube frames, so YouTube uses its plain-stream path (the one it uses on iPhones without MSE), and each deck's sound comes from an `<audio>` element (the deck's "voice") playing the same stream URL. It is created when YouTube sets the video's source and replaced when the source changes;
- the voice plays while the host page wants the deck playing (`on`, set by START / PAUSE / STOP and cleared when the song ends), follows every seek YouTube makes (cue jumps, loops, scratch release) and the deck's tempo. It never calls `play()` on an ended element, which would restart the song. A newly cued song never replays the previous one: the host bumps `gen` on each load, and the frame drops the old voice and ignores the old URL until YouTube loads the new one;
- the voice is the clock: it reports its time, length and state to the host page (`voice` messages, four a second while playing), which sends those to the app instead of YouTube's own numbers. The muted video is nudged to the voice's time at most every three seconds when they drift more than a second apart; nothing audible depends on it;
- the voice is loaded with `crossorigin="anonymous"` so the crossfader can route it through a Web Audio gain node; if the stream refuses CORS it reloads without it and the crossfader can only mute that deck (below 2 %), because iOS ignores a media element's volume. HLS streams are never routed;
- if YouTube reports a playback error with MSE hidden (anything but its invalid, removed or not-embeddable codes), the host page switches to the older decoy path (`fmmse=1` in the player URL) for that and later players. There, a deck's element is unmuted only once its metadata shows no video track.

`document.hidden` and `visibilityState` always report visible and `visibilitychange` is swallowed inside the YouTube frames, so the player never pauses itself when the app is minimized.

The script was checked in headless Chromium with a stand-in YouTube frame (MSE hidden, voice playing, seeks, pause, tempo, end without looping, a new song never replaying the old one, crossfader routing, background hand-off, scratch hold). Desktop browsers do not enforce the iOS rule, so the last check is on a phone.

### Playing in the background

`UIBackgroundModes` includes `audio`, and the audio session stays active while a deck plays, so the decks keep playing when the app is minimized or the phone is locked: an `<audio>` voice is allowed to play in the background, and if WebKit pauses the muted video the voice keeps going because it follows `on`, not the video. Web Audio is the exception: WebKit suspends every `AudioContext` in the background. When the app enters the background, `DjPlayerHost` calls `fmBackground(1)` inside a short background task; a frame whose voice is routed through the fader swaps it for a fresh, unrouted voice at the same position, and a deck on the decoy path is rebuilt as a fresh player without Web Audio (`bg` in the frame message stops it routing again). While minimized, the crossfader can only mute a deck. On returning, `fmBackground(0)` lets the decks route again.

Since 2026-10-18 switching out of the app no longer stutters. The old hand-off started the unrouted voice only when the app was already in the background, and by then WebKit had suspended Web Audio (`MediaSessionManageriOS::resetRestrictions` gives the `WebAudio` type `BackgroundProcessPlaybackRestricted`), so the music stopped while the fresh voice buffered from the network. After any crossfade or AUTOMIX both decks stayed routed, so this happened almost every time. The frame now keeps a **standby voice** (`standby` in `frameScript`), a muted `<audio>` of the same stream that plays alongside a routed voice:

- it exists only while the deck is routed, playing, not muted, not being scratched and in the foreground, and either at full gain or while the app is leaving (`willResignActive` sends `fmAway(1)`, `didBecomeActive` sends `fmAway(0)`, and `away` travels in the frame message). A deck held part-way on the fader therefore downloads its stream twice only from the moment the app starts to leave, not the whole time;
- it starts 0.2 s ahead and closes the gap by playing up to 15 % slower or faster (it is muted, so this cannot be heard). It seeks again only when it is more than 0.6 s out, and at most once every 2 s;
- once the deck is back at full gain and the standby is within 30 ms, the standby is unmuted and the routed voice is stopped and disconnected (`swap`). The deck is then unrouted, so going to the background has nothing to hand off. The next fade routes it again, because the standby also loads with CORS;
- on entering the background, `rescue` unmutes the standby if there is one (seeking it first if it is more than 80 ms out) and only builds a fresh voice when there is none. If the standby fails to load, the deck stops making standbys (`fmLone`) instead of retrying every tick.

The `source` message for the waveform is sent only for a deck's first voice, not for a standby. The decoy path (a routed MediaSource video) still rebuilds its player on entering the background.

### Reset All

The reset button (circular arrow, at the right of the bottom row) and the info sheet offer **Reset All** after a confirmation. It stops and unloads both decks, reloads the player web view so both YouTube players start from scratch, and puts cues, loops, tempo, bend, BPM, levels, crossfader, sampler level and samples back to their defaults, then saves that state. Recently Played is kept (it has its own Clear), and so are playlists and tags. A deck's loop is also no longer restored when the app is relaunched, so a song never starts out looping an 8-second section left over from an earlier session.

## Web and desktop

The studio accepts a YouTube URL or video id for each deck, detects the common watch, share, Shorts, live, embed, attribution and playlist forms, removes their extra parameters, embeds the canonical video through YouTube’s privacy-enhanced host, and uses the player message protocol for transport, position, tempo and volume. Its in-studio search gets results from a healthy public CORS-enabled Invidious index; selecting a result loads its canonical YouTube video directly into the chosen deck. Each overlay announces its current purpose and holds keyboard focus until it is closed, when focus returns to the control that opened it; selected search destinations and changing result states are also exposed to assistive technology. The equal-power crossfader sets each player’s effective volume and its matching opacity, so sound and picture make the same transition. Each deck’s touch platter pauses, seeks as it is rotated and resumes when released. Capture stores an eight-second source region as a local sampler section; its button seeks and plays the region back from its source deck. The local Web Audio sampler also has eight touch-safe synthesized percussion pads (mapped to `E`, `R`, `T`, `Y`, `U`, `I`, `O` and `P`), so it does not download, upload or retain audio recordings. Cues, captured regions, deck level, tempo, sampler level and the crossfader are retained only in `localStorage`, together with each deck's position and whether it was playing (2026-10-17): the position is saved every five seconds while a deck plays, on every play or pause and when the page is left, and reopening the page loads each deck at that position and starts the decks that were playing (the browser may hold back sound until the page has been touched). A page restored from the back-forward cache starts the decks that were playing when it was left. The web page does not use the jam worker yet; jam sessions are on iOS.

The page is intentionally controlled by the selected video’s availability policy. YouTube advertising, embedding rules and any playback restrictions remain under YouTube and the video owner’s control.
