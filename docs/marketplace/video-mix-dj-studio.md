# Video Mix DJ Studio

`video-mix-dj-studio` is an account-synced Marketplace package with a responsive dashboard page at `/dashboard/video-mix-dj-studio`, a desktop rail link, and a native iOS route. The dashboard page's mobile-first deck is a fixed, non-scrolling surface: both turntables and their waveforms remain visible in portrait and landscape, while loading, search, mixer, sampler and set actions open compact overlays above it. Loaded video artwork becomes the spinning record label.

## iOS

The iOS route is a SwiftUI studio, not a web page (`native/ios/ForkMesh/Features/Packages/VideoMixDjStudio*.swift`, with its pure logic and tests in `ForkMeshCore/Sources/FMPackages/DjStudio.swift`). Opening the route presents it as a full-screen cover, so the shell's top bar, tab bar, prompt bar, spider and every other overlay are hidden along with the status bar and home indicator. The back button in the top-left corner (also the gear menu and the info sheet) closes it in one tap and pops the route.

The studio model is app-wide (`DjStudioHub`), so leaving does not stop the music: playing decks keep playing and the shell's top bar shows a small spinning record (`DjNowPlayingChip`, `VideoMixDjStudioNowPlaying.swift`) with the loudest playing deck's artwork and a live seven-bar waveform of the playhead in its centre. Tapping it reopens the studio; long-press offers Stop the Music. Leaving with nothing playing hands the audio session back.

- **Landscape** follows the classic two-turntable layout: a top bar with the back button, each deck's elapsed time, title and channel, the menu, capture (record) and info buttons, and both waveforms; pitch strips on the outer edges (settings, BPM, SYNC, pitch fader, loop, − / + bend); the two platters with the pads button and AUTOMIX tab between them; and a bottom bar with each deck's START / PAUSE / STOP / RESTART and SET / cue / SAMPLE buttons around the crossfader.
- **Portrait** stacks Deck A above Deck B with the mixer (menu, capture, info, waveform zoom, pads, crossfader and AUTOMIX) between them; each deck has START / PAUSE / STOP / RESTART and SET / cue / SAMPLE down its left side.
- START plays a deck (or opens the loader when it is empty), PAUSE holds it where it is, STOP stops it and goes back to the beginning, and RESTART starts the song over from the beginning, so the transport is never a guess. PAUSE lights while a deck is paused mid-song and STOP while it is stopped at the start. SAMPLE saves eight seconds of that deck, from the current position, into the next sampler section.
- The iPhone app is otherwise portrait-only. `FmOrientationLock` (answered by `AppDelegate`'s `supportedInterfaceOrientationsFor`) allows landscape only while the studio is on screen and rotates back to portrait when it closes; iPad keeps every orientation.

Both decks play through YouTube IFrame players in one WKWebView (`DjPlayerHost`) hosted on the server's origin (or `https://forkmesh.com` when signed out), so YouTube's embedding rules still apply. The web view takes no touches and is parked behind the app's root view in the key window, so it keeps playing after the studio closes; the record shows the video's artwork, dimmed with the crossfader and turning with the platter.

YouTube's API only offers its own speed steps (0.25 apart) and iOS ignores a web player's volume, so a user script (`frameScript`, injected into the YouTube frames with `forMainFrameOnly: false`) works on the frame's `<video>` element directly, driven by `postMessage` from the host page:

- it sets `playbackRate` to the deck's tempo (with `preservesPitch` off, like vinyl) and puts it back whenever YouTube resets it, so the pitch fader, bend and SYNC change the music;
- once the crossfader or deck level drops below full, it routes the video through a Web Audio gain node, so the fader really fades. It only does this when the source is a `blob:` (MediaSource) stream, because a cross-origin stream would go silent; otherwise the old behaviour remains and the deck mutes below 2 %;
- while a record is being scratched it plays the track forward at the hand's speed and seeks back for backward strokes.

Position comes from a quarter-second player tick and is interpolated on device at the deck's real rate. The playhead only advances once YouTube reports the video playing (not when START is pressed), small drift is eased out and jumps over 0.3 s snap (`DjStudio.trackedPosition`), so the waveform playhead stays on the music. A static view is redrawn only when its inputs change; the turntable, waveform and clock timelines pause while a deck is stopped.

- Dragging a record scratches it like vinyl, 1.8 seconds of track per revolution. The hand's speed (`DjStudio.scratchSpeed`) drives the video forward at that speed and a synthesized scratch voice (band-passed noise whose pitch follows the speed, `DjScratchVoice` on the sampler's `AVAudioEngine`), louder on backward strokes and scaled by the deck's fader. Holding the record still silences it; releasing resumes playback if the deck was playing. The red light below the platter returns the deck to its cue and stops it.
- YouTube does not expose audio data, so the waveform strips are a stable picture generated from the video id: they show position and let you tap or drag to seek, and the waveform button zooms them to a 16-second window around the playhead.
- The pitch fader covers ±16 % (top is slower, double-tap resets), − / + bend the tempo by 4 % while held, and the BPM box is tap tempo: tap it on the beat, then SYNC matches this deck's BPM (or, without BPMs, its tempo) to the other deck. Without a tapped BPM the box shows the tempo change as a percentage.
- The crossfader is equal power and fades through the Web Audio gain described above; its arrows slam to one side and AUTOMIX starts the idle deck, fades to it over eight seconds and pauses the other.
- The record button captures eight seconds from the deck you can hear into one of four sections; the sampler sheet replays them and has the eight synthesized drum pads, rendered once into `AVAudioEngine` buffers and triggered on touch-down.
- Search uses the same public Invidious index as the web page, trying up to three healthy instances. Loading is one step: type and press return, and the top result loads straight onto the deck and the sheet closes; a pasted YouTube link loads directly. Reopening the loader shows the other results (the loaded one ticked) to swap in a different video.
- Every song a deck starts playing goes to the top of **Recently Played** in the loader (the latest 40, no duplicates, titles filled in once YouTube reports them; `DjStudio.remember`). With the search box empty it is the first list, so a past song reloads in one tap; swipe a row to remove it or use Clear. It is kept with the rest of the studio state on the device.
- Deck videos, titles, cues, tempo, BPM, levels, sections, sampler level, crossfader, zoom and the Recently Played history are kept in `UserDefaults` (`fm.videoMixDjStudio.v1`) on the device only. While the studio is open the screen stays awake and the audio session mixes the decks with the sampler.

### Why both decks can play at once

On iOS, WebKit lets only one `<video>` with sound play at a time across the whole app: starting one pauses every other one (`MediaSessionManageriOS::resetRestrictions` gives the `VideoAudio` media type `ConcurrentPlaybackNotPermitted`). The same type is also paused when the app goes to the background. A muted `<video>` is the `Video` type and an `<audio>` element is always the `Audio` type (`HTMLMediaElement::hasVideo` is only true for `<video>`), and neither has that rule.

Build 260 still stopped one deck when the other started. Its fix (2026-10-16) moved each deck's video track onto a hidden decoy `MediaSource`, which only works when YouTube streams through Media Source Extensions; when YouTube used a plain or HLS stream the deck's element still had video and sound, so the rule applied. The decoy also never played, so its buffer was never released, which made YouTube rebuffer and jump back (the "looping"). Since 2026-10-16 (second fix) the frame script makes the restriction impossible to hit:

- every YouTube `<video>` is kept muted for real. YouTube still sees its own mute setting, because `muted` is wrapped to report what YouTube last set. The mute is applied before any `play()` and again on `loadstart`, so no YouTube element can ever be `VideoAudio`;
- `MediaSource` and `ManagedMediaSource` are hidden inside the YouTube frames, so YouTube uses its plain-stream path (the one it uses on iPhones without MSE), and each deck's sound comes from an `<audio>` element (the deck's "voice") playing the same stream URL. It is created when YouTube sets the video's source and replaced when the source changes;
- the voice plays while the host page wants the deck playing (`on`, set by START / PAUSE / STOP and cleared when the song ends), follows every seek YouTube makes (cue jumps, loops, scratch release) and the deck's tempo. It never calls `play()` on an ended element, which would restart the song. A newly cued song never replays the previous one: the host bumps `gen` on each load, and the frame drops the old voice and ignores the old URL until YouTube loads the new one;
- the voice is the clock: it reports its time, length and state to the host page (`voice` messages, four a second while playing), which sends those to the app instead of YouTube's own numbers. The muted video is nudged to the voice's time at most every three seconds when they drift more than a second apart; nothing audible depends on it;
- the voice is loaded with `crossorigin="anonymous"` so the crossfader can route it through a Web Audio gain node; if the stream refuses CORS it reloads without it and the crossfader can only mute that deck (below 2 %), because iOS ignores a media element's volume. HLS streams are never routed;
- if YouTube reports a playback error with MSE hidden (anything but its invalid, removed or not-embeddable codes), the host page switches to the older decoy path (`fmmse=1` in the player URL) for that and later players. There, a deck's element is unmuted only once its metadata shows no video track.

`document.hidden` and `visibilityState` always report visible and `visibilitychange` is swallowed inside the YouTube frames, so the player never pauses itself when the app is minimized.

The script was checked in headless Chromium with a stand-in YouTube frame (MSE hidden, voice playing, seeks, pause, tempo, end without looping, a new song never replaying the old one, crossfader routing, background hand-off, scratch hold). Desktop browsers do not enforce the iOS rule, so the last check is on a phone.

### Playing in the background

`UIBackgroundModes` includes `audio`, and the audio session stays active while a deck plays, so the decks keep playing when the app is minimized or the phone is locked: an `<audio>` voice is allowed to play in the background, and if WebKit pauses the muted video the voice keeps going because it follows `on`, not the video. Web Audio is the exception: WebKit suspends every `AudioContext` in the background. When the app enters the background, `DjPlayerHost` calls `fmBackground(1)` inside a short background task; a frame whose voice is routed through the fader swaps it for a fresh, unrouted voice at the same position, and a deck on the decoy path is rebuilt as a fresh player without Web Audio (`bg` in the frame message stops it routing again). While minimized, the crossfader can only mute a deck. On returning, `fmBackground(0)` lets the decks route again.

### Reset All

The reset button (circular arrow, beside the info button), the gear menu and the info sheet offer **Reset All** after a confirmation. It stops and unloads both decks, reloads the player web view so both YouTube players start from scratch, and puts cues, loops, tempo, bend, BPM, levels, crossfader, sampler level, sections and waveform zoom back to their defaults, then saves that state. Recently Played is kept (it has its own Clear). A deck's loop is also no longer restored when the app is relaunched, so a song never starts out looping an 8-second section left over from an earlier session.

## Web and desktop

The studio accepts a YouTube URL or video id for each deck, detects the common watch, share, Shorts, live, embed, attribution and playlist forms, removes their extra parameters, embeds the canonical video through YouTube’s privacy-enhanced host, and uses the player message protocol for transport, position, tempo and volume. Its in-studio search gets results from a healthy public CORS-enabled Invidious index; selecting a result loads its canonical YouTube video directly into the chosen deck. Each overlay announces its current purpose and holds keyboard focus until it is closed, when focus returns to the control that opened it; selected search destinations and changing result states are also exposed to assistive technology. The equal-power crossfader sets each player’s effective volume and its matching opacity, so sound and picture make the same transition. Each deck’s touch platter pauses, seeks as it is rotated and resumes when released. Capture stores an eight-second source region as a local sampler section; its button seeks and plays the region back from its source deck. The local Web Audio sampler also has eight touch-safe synthesized percussion pads (mapped to `E`, `R`, `T`, `Y`, `U`, `I`, `O` and `P`), so it does not download, upload or retain audio recordings. Cues, captured regions, deck level, tempo, sampler level and the crossfader are retained only in `localStorage`; no package API or worker is present.

The page is intentionally controlled by the selected video’s availability policy. YouTube advertising, embedding rules and any playback restrictions remain under YouTube and the video owner’s control.
